# letter-garden behavior

- Enter seven distinct ASCII letters in one text field, center letter first. The first letter typed is the required letter; no separate control picks it. Lowercase input and strip everything but letters, noting when non-space characters are ignored and when a letter repeats. Reject any normalized length other than seven and any repeated letter, with the error beside the field.
- Mirror the field in letter squares: seven at first, the first marked as the center, more drawn (up to 21) when a paste runs long. Mark repeated letters and letters past the seventh as errors, and the center square when the required letter is the problem. A count shows letters typed out of seven.
- Submit to a shareable URL such as `/results?letters=acinort&required=c`. Canonical URLs sort and lowercase the letters. Valid noncanonical URLs are replaced in place. Invalid URLs show a "Check your letters" form holding the link's letters as given, with the error, and fetch no word data.
- Find words of four or more letters using only the seven allowed letters and including the required one. Letters may repeat. Show pangrams, the words using all seven, first, then the remaining words by length, longest first. Sort words alphabetically within each list. Show 50 at first, and move focus to the first new word when showing the rest.
- Each section heading shows its own word count; an empty section says so in a note instead. The total is announced to screen readers but not shown, since a general dictionary's total rarely matches the game's.
- Build a compact trie from the vendored wamerican list. Keep lowercase ASCII words with at most seven distinct letters. The home page fetches no word data. Results announce loading and completion, offer retry on failure, and explain when there are no matches.
- The results page shows the puzzle as squares, center first and the rest alphabetical, with a pencil to edit them in place. Escape and Cancel close the editor and return focus to the pencil.
- Use the shared masthead and theme toggle, with the form in one column. Both pages explain the dictionary's limits and link to the source. No scores, hints, or game-specific word-list claims.
